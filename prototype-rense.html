Site Architecture

Four pillars in the top-level structure. Heritage is not a fifth pillar: it is a cross-cutting collection tag that lives inside The Collection's product taxonomy, and a storytelling strand inside The Studio. This keeps the nav restrained while letting one product carry both an object-type and a narrative.

01
Home SEE PROTOTYPE TAB
Visual storytelling entry point. One module links out to the Heritage story; category tiles route into The Collection; product grid is not shown here.
02
Shop The Collection
Permanent, always-on inventory. Organised by object-type, not by story.
Objects
Stationery, ceramics, small desk items. Commodity + Heritage pieces sit in the same grid, same card size.
Wearables
Jackets, clothing. Filterable by Heritage tag alongside standard sort options.
Space
Furniture. Same rule: Heritage is a badge, not a separate shelf.
Filter: Heritage TAG, NOT A PAGE
A pill filter across all three types: surfaces every purchasable Heritage piece without giving it its own top-level destination.
03
Editions / Drops
Limited-run, high-urgency releases. Separate release logic from The Collection. Sold-out items move to Archive rather than being deleted. Links out to active Kickstarter campaigns.
04
The Studio
Portfolio/blog hybrid: prototyping, CAD, manufacturer visits, and the long-form Heritage narrative.
Heritage, Purpose, Function STORY HOME
The long-form personal narrative lives here: upbringing, philosophy, the "why" behind specific pieces. Each entry deep-links to the purchasable product page, driving traffic into the shop rather than dead-ending as a blog.
05
Trade / Wholesale FOOTER ONLY
Not in top nav. Footer link only: line sheets, MOQs, B2B portal login for buyers.
Why Heritage isn't a top-level tab: Objects / Wearables / Space answer "what is it," Heritage answers "why does it exist." Making Heritage a peer category forces every product into an either/or decision it doesn't need to make. As a tag + a Studio story instead, a product can be an Object and a Heritage piece: badge on the card, collapsible story on the product page, full narrative in The Studio.

› Reproduced from a stool that sat by my nan's fire. Click to read.

Growing up, this stool lived by the hearth in my grandmother's front room, the seat you were given if you were the last one to arrive. When I started working with the joiner in Herefordshire, I brought in a photo and we rebuilt it from memory and measurement, keeping the slightly uneven turn on the legs rather than machining it smooth.
